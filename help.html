Getting started

Open your manuscript, then Extensions → WriteFlow → Open WriteFlow. The first time, Google asks for permission to let WriteFlow read this document and show its sidebar.

Use Google Docs' Heading 1 (or Heading 2) style for chapter titles. That's how WriteFlow finds your chapters. Without headings it guesses from lines like "Chapter One".

Chapters and Go to

The Chapters tab lists every chapter with its word count and a bar comparing its length to the others. Click one to jump there. The one you're in is highlighted.

The Go to box at the top of Navigate finds chapters, Story Bible entries and pins, and runs commands: start a sprint, switch stage, back up, open Night Reader, change theme and more. Type part of a name, use ↑ ↓, then Enter. If a command can't run right now, it says why. Anything you type can also be searched for in the manuscript.

Shortcut: while you're in the sidebar, press Ctrl+Shift+K (⌘+Shift+K on Mac) or / to jump to Go to. Google Docs doesn't let add-ons listen to keys typed in the document itself, so click into the sidebar first.

Pins mark a spot you keep returning to: put your cursor there and choose "Pin where my cursor is". Pins still find their spot after you edit around them.

Skip (shown when you hover over a chapter) leaves notes, outlines or cut scenes out of your word count.

Search

Typing never moves your document. Results appear in the sidebar with the word highlighted. Press Enter for the next match after your cursor, Shift+Enter for the previous one. You also see how often you use the word, where it's most frequent, and the closest cluster.

Search my selection searches whatever you've selected in the document. There's also a menu item for it: Extensions → WriteFlow → Find selected text.

Story Bible and Codex

Groups it recognises (by the Heading 2 name): Characters (or Cast, People), Places (Locations, Settings, Cities), Objects (Artefacts, Items, Weapons), Factions (Houses, Guilds, Families, Organisations), World rules (Magic system, Worldbuilding, Laws, Lore), Events (History, Backstory), Creatures, and Terms (Glossary). Anything else is listed as other entries. Places, objects, factions and the rest get mention counts and "Appears in" like characters; world rules show the rule itself instead, because a rule isn't a name in your text.

Add a tab named Story Bible (or a Heading 1 called Story Bible at the end of a one-tab document). Inside it:

Characters                         ← Heading 2 (a group)
Anna Reyes (Annie)                 ← Heading 3 (an entry; "Annie" is another name)
Also: Ms Reyes, the keeper's girl  ← more names
Role: Daughter · Age: 34           ← details
Afraid of deep water.              ← notes
Places                             ← Heading 2
Saltmarsh Point                    ← Heading 3

The Codex tab shows who appears in the chapter you're in, every entry's mention count, and who hasn't appeared for a while. Matching is case-sensitive, so "Will" the character isn't confused with "will". Characters automatically get their first name as another name unless it's shared or an everyday word. The Story Bible is never counted in your word count.

Insights

Sentence length and variety, sentence openers, paragraph length, vocabulary diversity, most-used words, echoes, adverbs, passive voice, dialogue and reading ease, for the whole book or one chapter. Tap a highlighted item to jump to it. Compare chapters marks chapters that are far from your own average. These are pattern counts, not grades.

Table and Structure

In your Story Bible, add a Heading 2 called Chapters, then a Heading 3 per chapter with a line like POV: Anna · Status: Draft · Day: 4 · Beat: Midpoint. WriteFlow matches these to your chapters by title or number.

Chapters → Table groups, filters and sorts chapters by these details. "Open wide table" shows them all, with a CSV download. Chapters → Structure lays a beat sheet over your book and shows where each beat would typically fall.

Goals and sprints

Set a daily goal, a total length and a deadline under Home → Goals and history. A day counts from when you first open WriteFlow that day. Sprints run from Home. The countdown shows on every tab, and the final count is exact.

Where you stopped

While the sidebar is open, WriteFlow remembers where you were writing. Continue writing on Home takes you straight back. Changes a collaborator makes elsewhere are ignored.

Lifeboat (offline)

If your connection drops while the sidebar is open, you can still read your chapters, write in a scratchpad saved in your browser, and download a copy. When you're back online, WriteFlow copies the scratchpad and takes your cursor to where you stopped so you can paste it.

Writer's Desk

Your private start page. It opens instantly and shows the document you were working on, the chapter and last lines where you stopped, today's goal and your streak. Find its link on Home under Share and export, or in Extensions → WriteFlow → Help and privacy, and bookmark it.

Start each session there: Ctrl+click (⌘+click on Mac) Open my novel so Google Docs loads in a background tab while you read. Then switch over and press Continue writing.

Only you can see it. To leave your lines off the Desk, turn off "Show the last few lines I wrote on the Desk" in Settings. Use Hide to remove an old document from it.

Night Reader

A large, dark window for re-reading (Home → Night Reader). Choose Night, Dim or Day and a text size. Click any paragraph to move your cursor there in the document.

Share and export

Snippet turns a selected passage into an image. Writing Journey makes a one-page report of your writing history. Export to Obsidian downloads a vault with chapters, Story Bible notes and links. Backup saves your manuscript as text or Markdown.

How words are counted

A word is a run of letters or digits, joined by an apostrophe or hyphen: "don't" is one word, "well-known" is one, "word—word" (em dash) is two. Headings count. The Story Bible and skipped chapters don't. Tables, headers, footers and footnotes aren't included yet, which is the usual reason a count differs slightly from Tools → Word count.

Storage and backup

More → Storage and backup shows what WriteFlow keeps: project data in this browser, and goals, history and pins in your Google account for each document, with a Forget button for each. Download backup saves one file for this document (never the manuscript itself, which stays in Google Docs). Restore from a backup shows what the file contains first; restoring adds what's missing and never deletes anything or replaces newer work. Project data lives in this browser only, so back up before switching computers.

Privacy

WriteFlow only opens the document you start it in, never edits it, and doesn't send your text anywhere. Settings → Forget this document deletes everything it saved for that document. Full details: privacy policy.

Troubleshooting

Google says "Google hasn't verified this app"

During the beta, copies of WriteFlow haven't been through Google's review yet. Choose Advanced to continue. The published version is reviewed by Google.

The sidebar takes a long time to open

The first open reads the whole document. After that it opens instantly from a saved copy and only re-reads what changed. Very long documents (200,000+ words) take longer. Send us Settings → Diagnostics so we can see the timings.

A jump landed in the wrong place

Press the refresh icon at the top of the sidebar and try again. If it happens repeatedly, email us with what you clicked and your Diagnostics.

My word count differs from Tools → Word count

See how words are counted. Hyphenated words, tables and footnotes are the usual causes.

My chapters aren't listed

Apply Heading 1 or Heading 2 to your chapter titles (Format → Paragraph styles). Empty headings are ignored.

A character shows 0 mentions

Check the spelling and capitalisation, and add the names you actually use with an "Also:" line. Mentions in skipped chapters and the Story Bible itself aren't counted.

The Lifeboat didn't appear when I went offline

It only works if the sidebar was already open. Google Docs won't open add-ons without a connection.
